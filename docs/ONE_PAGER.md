# Where have Mauritius' rare animals been recorded?

**Why.** Mauritius is home to birds, bats and reptiles found nowhere else, and many of them are rare. Protecting them starts with knowing where they have been seen, and whether those places are protected.

**What I did.** I collected open observation records of twelve endemic or threatened animals from public biodiversity databases and cleaned them with documented rules. That left 4,626 records of nine species. I linked each record to the type of land it was found on and to the island's protected areas, and compared where animals were recorded with how much of the island's tree cover is protected.

**What I found.**
- Most records come from very few sources and places. One bird-watching platform supplies 82% of the data, and a single location supplies 13%.
- 89% of the records are on tree-covered land, and 46.5% lie inside protected areas, although only 7.7% of the island's tree cover and 4.3% of all its land is protected.
- The picture differs by species. 85% of the Mauritius cuckooshrike records are inside protected areas, but only 8% for the ornate day gecko. Three of the twelve species have too few usable records, because their locations are blurred or imprecise.

**What this does not show.** Records show where people looked, not where animals live, so busy trails and well-known sites are over-represented. Tree cover also includes plantations and forest overgrown by exotic plants, so it is not the same as native forest, and the protected share of tree cover is not the share of native forest that is protected.

**What could be next.** Add the islands of Rodrigues and Réunion, and look at how forest changes over the years, to see whether protection keeps pace.

*Data: GBIF (retrieved 2026-11-10), Protected Planet (WDPA, Nov 2026), ESA WorldCover 2021, IUCN Red List. Details, methods and limits are in the repository README.*
